## Introduction

I was speaking with a good friend recently who communicated to me their frustrations with their current job. They did like the work or industry, didn't particularly enjoy being around the people, and their manager was making them miserable. After a bit of back and forth, they described how they have been struggling to find their passion vis a vi work. They couldn't figure out what they were _supposed_ to do, what role they were _meant_ to fill, and it was weighing on them. Far be it from me to tell someone what the meaning of their life is, but what I do know is that I had a similar journey not too long ago. I thought I had found a job in an industry that was my passion in life. When it came to bear that this job could not sustain me financially- much less a family- I had nothing short of an identity crisis. Over the years I have reflected on that period of my life. Why was that transition so difficult for me? While I still look back fondly on my time in that industry, calling it my life's passion seems like nothing short of hyperbole today.

One day I would like to explore why this framing of work exists, but that's a topic for another article. This is meant to codify the advice I gave my good friend which he seems to have thought was helpful.

## Work you love vs. work you dont hate

The first bit of advice I gave him was to lower his expectations with his career transition. Don't look for a job that gives you meaning, or that is the perfect combination of all of your hobbies and interests. Look for a job in an industry that overlaps with the skills you already have, that you could see yourself going into every day and not hating. The job that combines all your passions, interests, and skills likely doesn't exist, doesn't pay well, or is saturated with everyone else whose interests overlap.
