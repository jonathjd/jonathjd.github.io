Starting a research career can feel overwhelming. There are so many papers to read, techniques to learn, and ideas to explore. Here are some thoughts that I've found helpful.

## Finding Good Problems

The most important skill in research is learning to identify problems worth solving. A good problem has several characteristics:

- **It matters**: The solution would be useful to someone
- **It's tractable**: There's a reasonable path to making progress
- **It's interesting**: You'll enjoy thinking about it

Don't worry too much about finding the "perfect" problem. Most successful researchers stumble into good problems through a combination of curiosity and persistence.

## Reading Papers

When starting out, it's tempting to try to read everything. This is a mistake. Instead:

1. Read broadly at first to get a sense of the field
2. Then dive deep into a few papers that excite you
3. Understand those papers completely—implement the methods, reproduce the results

A few papers read deeply are worth more than dozens skimmed.

## Writing

Writing is thinking. Start writing early, even before you have results. The act of explaining your ideas will clarify them. Keep a research notebook where you write down:

- Questions you're trying to answer
- Experiments you've run and what you learned
- Ideas for next steps

This notebook will become invaluable when it comes time to write papers.

## Final Thoughts

Research is a long game. Be patient with yourself, stay curious, and don't be afraid to ask questions. The best researchers I know are also the most willing to admit what they don't understand.
